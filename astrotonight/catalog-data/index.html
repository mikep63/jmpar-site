What AstroTonight changes at import

AstroTonight does not alter these source files. Instead, at catalog import time it applies a documented set of corrections and additions — cross- referencing other public catalogs, fixing a small number of errors, and supplementing common names. The complete, structured list of every change is published here:

modifications.json

In summary, the modifications are:

  • Caldwell catalogue numbers — OpenNGC does not include Caldwell numbers; 105 are added by cross-reference, including two (C37, C50) redirected to the surviving object where OpenNGC's own data marks the traditional designation as a duplicate.
  • Messier 102 — OpenNGC marks M102 as a duplicate of M101 with no data of its own. Modern consensus (SIMBAD/NED) identifies it as NGC 5866; that assignment is added back.
  • IC 1005's NGC number — OpenNGC marks NGC 5620 as a duplicate of IC 1005, and the surviving entry carries no NGC cross-reference of its own; the NGC designation is recorded on it so the object appears under both catalogues.
  • One type-code correction — NGC 6611 (M16) is tagged as a nebula in OpenNGC; it is the star cluster (IC 4703 is the nebula it illuminates), matching SIMBAD/NED classification.
  • Common names — 45 Messier objects, 28 Caldwell objects, and 42 other objects gain common names absent from OpenNGC (sourced from Wikipedia's Messier/Caldwell tables, or well-established astrophotography usage), appended after any name OpenNGC already lists.
  • Herschel 400 program — encoded as a set of 402 NGC numbers (the program has 400 entries; three are traditionally listed under a pair of numbers for the same object). Six further entries are redirected to their surviving non-duplicate counterpart where OpenNGC marks the traditional number as a duplicate.
  • Cross-catalog merges — a small number of Sharpless and RCW entries (6 total) are identified as the same physical object as an existing NGC/IC entry and merged rather than listed twice, where the automatic cross-reference detection didn't catch it.

Other catalogs used

AstroTonight also bundles data from these catalogues, obtained via the VizieR catalogue access tool, CDS, Strasbourg, France (DOI: 10.26093/cds/vizier). The original description of the VizieR service was published in Ochsenbein, F., Bauer, P., Marcout, J. 2000, A&AS 143, 23.

  • Sharpless catalog (VizieR VII/20): Sharpless, S. 1959, Catalogue of HII Regions, ApJS 4, 257.
  • RCW catalogue (VizieR VII/216): Rodgers, A. W., Campbell, C. T., Whiteoak, J. B. 1960, H-α emission regions in the Southern Milky Way, MNRAS 121, 103.
  • Lynds' Catalogue of Dark Nebulae (VizieR VII/7): Lynds, B. T. 1962, ApJS 7, 1.
  • Barnard catalogue (VizieR VII/220): Barnard, E. E. 1927, A Photographic Atlas of Selected Regions of the Milky Way.
  • Yale Bright Star Catalogue, 5th ed. (VizieR V/50), for Sky View's stars: Hoffleit, D., Jaschek, C. 1991.

Sky View's constellation figures use the ConstellationLines dataset, © 2005–2026 Marc van der Sluys, hemel.waarnemen.com, licensed CC BY 4.0. AstroTonight converts it to its own format (lists of Bright Star Catalogue numbers); the figures themselves are unchanged.

AstroTonight is free and has no ads or purchases. Unlike the catalog files above, the thumbnails may not be used commercially.
